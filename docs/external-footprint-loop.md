# Signalpost external-footprint loop

## Competition objective

The differentiator is not access to Norway's public company registry. It is the agent's ability to
resolve the exact legal entity across external sources, collect useful dated signals, and turn them
into a compact, evidence-backed view without confusing a parent, franchise, brand, location, or namesake.

This document is an engineering research plan, **not** the competition scoring rubric. The official
[Signalpost challenge page](https://builderr.ai/challenges/signalpost), checked 2026-10-16, sets the
qualification bar at 65/100 on an official run and scores recall/coverage (50), precision/evidence (30),
synthesis (12), and UX (8). Within recall/coverage, the page describes a 70% company-coverage and 30%
checked-fact component for each information type. Builderr supplies the fresh evaluation batch. Local
connector metrics below are diagnostics only and cannot predict official points.

The following are internal connector evaluation measures, not official score categories:

| Internal metric | What to report |
|---|---|
| Exact-entity attribution | Precision and abstentions |
| Source breadth | Distinct verified source classes |
| Workforce/jobs | Checked fact coverage and freshness |
| Ratings/reviews | Only where permitted and independently verified |
| Buzz/engagement | Source-specific metrics; do not blend unlike metrics |
| Freshness | Retrieval time and source reporting period |

For each connector, report both (a) verified findings against an independent held-out label set and
(b) observed population reach, with the denominator and sampling method stated. These internal measures
must not be presented as Builderr's hidden score. Missing or blocked data is not silently converted to a
negative/neutral fact, and a system must not define its own gold denominator from its output.

## Collection loop

1. Start with organisation number, legal name, aliases, registered address, phone, domain, and subunits.
2. Discover candidate website, place, social, review, news, and job URLs from the verified company site,
   permitted search APIs, platform APIs, and licensed providers.
3. Score exact-entity identity using organisation number first, then domain, address/phone, legal name,
   and cross-links. Ambiguous candidates abstain.
4. Run a connector only when its acquisition mode and evidence-retention policy are approved. Store raw
   capture hash, URL, retrieval time, source class, connector version, and exact evidence span.
5. Normalize observations without blending unlike metrics. A Google rating, Glassdoor employee rating,
   LinkedIn followers, YouTube views, and news mentions remain separately labelled.
6. Compute company-level buzz/sentiment only when the minimum independent evidence gate passes. Otherwise
   show dated item-level signals or `not available`.
7. Refresh 100 frozen random companies each day. Compare changes, retries, cost, p50/p95 latency, and
   source-specific availability. Never convert a blocked or missing connector into a zero signal.
8. Blind-label a rotating sample. These are proposed internal release gates, not Builderr thresholds:
   >=99.5% exact-entity precision, >=98% metric correctness, complete evidence, and zero
   wrong-company publications on the audited sample. Report sample size and uncertainty.

## Fuzzy identity graph

Fuzzy discovery may expand recall, but never weakens publication identity:

1. Begin with the legal name, organisation number, registry roles, municipality, and any exact official site.
2. Derive public-brand aliases only from the verified site title or structured `Organization` name.
3. Query company discovery with the legal name and those site-backed aliases. Retain candidates, not facts.
4. Query registered leaders/founders against the public brand. A personal profile is corroboration only when
   the exact person and current company/founder relationship are both visible.
5. Reverse-resolve every candidate to the Norwegian entity using the official domain, municipality/address,
   a registered leader, or an exact company-ID-to-job-page link. Name similarity alone abstains.
6. Treat a LinkedIn website as a new site candidate. Independently crawl that domain, rerun the normal
   website identity gate, and only then publish its contact, location, and social cross-links.
7. Feed newly verified social handles back into their platform connectors and repeat until a full pass adds
   no new exact sites, handles, profiles, jobs, or dated activity.

The implemented provider-neutral leader/trade-name queue is generated by
`scripts/build_linkedin_identity_graph_tasks.py`. Query rows are not evidence and cannot score until an
approved search provider returns retained captures that pass the reverse-proof gate.

## Historical LinkedIn experiment (2026-08-23; not current submission evidence)

The figures below are historical project notes. They are not an official benchmark result, do not establish
current reproducibility, and do not establish permission to collect or retain LinkedIn data. The raw
artifacts and authorization would need to be checked before relying on them.

- Legal-name-only discovery: 7 exact profiles from the frozen 1,000.
- Official-site alias discovery plus reverse proof: 32 exact profiles after resumable retries.
- Profile extraction: 32/32, producing 32 profile summaries, 29 associated-workforce snapshots and
  103 recent company posts.
- Company-ID job discovery: 2 exact current jobs for one company; all retained detail pages linked the
  exact company URL.
- LinkedIn website feedback: 11 new website candidates, 9 independently verified exact sites, and 17 new
  official-site social handles.
- Full-1,000 experimental completeness >=65: 28 companies before the loop, 45 after it.

These are POC coverage results, not permission to republish LinkedIn data. Automated LinkedIn captures
remain experimental pending platform authorization; independently verified company-site outputs can pass
the normal strict evidence policy.

## Connector order

| Priority | Connector | Purpose | Gate before enabling |
|---|---|---|---|
| 1 | Verified company site | handles, contact, locations, canonical cross-links | permitted crawl + identity gate |
| 2 | Google Places API | place IDs, rating, rating count, limited reviews, locations | official API terms and attribution |
| 3 | YouTube Data API | channel identity, subscribers, views, video/post cadence | official API |
| 4 | Search/news provider | candidate handles and independent company mentions | licensed/permitted API + retained fetched evidence |
| 5 | Job feeds / JobSpy experiment | active jobs and hiring direction | provider-approved feed for scoring; JobSpy remains experimental |
| 6 | LinkedIn | company page and workforce/follower signals | official/partner/licensed or company-authorized access |
| 7 | Meta, Instagram, X, TikTok | public cadence and engagement | approved API/licensed access or permitted pages |
| 8 | Glassdoor/Indeed reviews | employee rating and themes | licensed/authorized access; no score from prohibited scraping |

JobSpy is MIT-licensed code, but the code license does not grant rights to target-platform data. It is
useful for a bounded jobs-coverage bake-off. Proxy bypass behavior, rate limits, and target terms must be
treated as deployment risks, and its results stay experimental until approved.

## Internal control rule (not an official scoring formula)

Baseline is the current 1,000-company snapshot. For each connector, run 100 development companies, label
the output, then run a zero-overlap 100-company validation set. Keep a connector only if it adds useful,
independently checked information while preserving zero wrong-company publication, evidence completeness,
source rights, bounded runtime, and declared cost. Treat any numerical uplift threshold as a local
experiment design choice, not a competition guarantee. After two iterations without improvement, stop and
record whether the blocker is access rights, source availability, entity resolution, or extraction.
